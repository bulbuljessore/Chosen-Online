How to write a CV that South African recruiters actually read

Most recruiters spend under a minute on a first read. Here’s what to put at the top, what to cut, and how to make your experience easy to scan.

Nomsa ZuluSenior Recruiter at CHOSEN · 2 October 2026
In this article {{ t.label }} {{ t.label }}

1. Put a short summary at the top

Three or four lines that say who you are, what you do and what you want next. “Junior data analyst with two years in logistics reporting, looking for a hybrid role in Gauteng” tells a recruiter more than a paragraph about being hard-working.

2. Lead each job with results

Under every role, list three to five bullet points. Start each one with what changed because of your work: “Cut monthly report time from two days to four hours” beats “Responsible for monthly reports”.

“If I can’t see your last job title and dates in the first ten seconds, I move on.”Recruiter, financial services, Johannesburg

3. Keep it to two pages

Unless you’re applying for an academic or senior executive role, two pages is enough. Drop your ID number, marital status and full home address. Your city and a phone number are all an employer needs at this stage.

IncludeCity and province · Phone and email · Work history with dates · Qualifications · Languages · Driver’s licence code
Leave outID number · Marital status · Religion · Full street address · Photos (unless asked) · References’ numbers

4. Match the words in the job ad

Many employers use software to sort CVs. If the ad asks for “Power BI”, write “Power BI”, not “dashboards”. Keep the layout simple: one column, standard headings, and save it as a PDF.
